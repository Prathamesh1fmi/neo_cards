# NeoCards User Guide

Welcome to the official NeoCards User Guide! This manual will teach you everything you need to know about NeoCards, step by step. 

---

## Chapter 1: Welcome to NeoCards

### What NeoCards Is
NeoCards is a smart flashcard program designed for your computer. It helps you remember anything you want to learn—from a new language to medical school vocabulary—by showing you flashcards right before you are about to forget them. 

### Who It Is For
NeoCards is for anyone who wants to learn faster and remember longer. Students, doctors, language learners, programmers, and trivia fans all use NeoCards to build their personal knowledge bases.

### Why Someone Should Use It
Human memory fades over time. If you learn a new word today, you will likely forget it by next week. NeoCards uses a mathematical formula (called FSRS) to predict exactly when you are about to forget a piece of information. It then tests you on that exact day. This saves you hundreds of hours of studying because you only study what you actually need to review.

### Main Features
- **Smart Scheduling**: The computer does the math to figure out when you need to review.
- **Rich Text Editor**: Write your notes using bold, colors, pictures, and math symbols.
- **Offline First**: You do not need the internet to study. Your flashcards live on your computer.
- **AI Assistant**: A built-in tutor that can help you write flashcards or summarize long notes.
- **Syncing**: Keep your flashcards safe and updated across multiple computers.

## Chapter 2: Installation

NeoCards is a desktop application. This means it is a program you download and install directly onto your computer, just like a web browser or a word processor.

### Windows
1. Go to the NeoCards website and click **Download for Windows**.
2. You will get a file that ends in `.msi` or `.exe`. Double-click this file.
3. Follow the instructions on your screen. Click **Next** until the installation finishes.
4. An icon for NeoCards will appear on your desktop. Double-click it to start.

### macOS
1. Go to the NeoCards website and click **Download for Mac**.
2. You will get a file that ends in `.dmg`. Double-click it to open it.
3. A window will pop up showing the NeoCards icon and your Applications folder. Drag the NeoCards icon into the Applications folder.
4. Open your Applications folder and double-click NeoCards to start. 
   - *Note*: The first time you open it, your Mac might ask if you are sure you want to open a program downloaded from the internet. Click **Open**.

### Linux
1. Go to the NeoCards website and click **Download for Linux**.
2. You will get a `.AppImage` file.
3. Right-click the file, go to **Properties**, go to the **Permissions** tab, and check the box that says "Allow executing file as program."
4. Double-click the file to run NeoCards.

### First Launch & Database Creation
When you open NeoCards for the very first time, it does a little bit of background work. 
It creates a hidden folder on your computer where it will safely store all of your flashcards, images, and statistics. 

- **What it does**: Creates a file called a "Database" (specifically, an SQLite database).
- **Why it exists**: To keep your data safe and incredibly fast to search.
- **When to use it**: It happens automatically! You don't have to do anything.
- **Common Mistakes**: Do not try to move the hidden database file yourself using your computer's file explorer. If you want to back up your data, use the Export feature inside NeoCards.

## Chapter 3: First Run Experience

### What the User Sees
When the loading screen finishes, you will see a clean, modern window. On the left, there is a sidebar with icons. In the center, you will see a welcome message and a big button that says **Create Deck**.

### Initial Setup
NeoCards does not force you to make an account. You can start using it immediately. 
If you want to use the Cloud Backup feature later, you can sign in, but for now, the software is ready to go.

### Creating Your First Profile
By default, you are using the "Default Profile." A profile is like a user account on your computer. 
- **Why it exists**: If you share your computer with a sibling, you can each have your own profile so your flashcards do not mix.
- **When to use it**: If multiple people use the same computer, or if you want to keep your "Work" flashcards completely separated from your "Hobby" flashcards.
- **When NOT to use it**: If you are the only person using the computer, just stick to the Default Profile.

### Understanding the Home Screen
The Home Screen is your daily starting point. 
- You will see a list of your "Decks" (folders that hold your flashcards).
- Next to each Deck, you will see numbers indicating how many cards you need to study today.
- If the numbers are 0, you are done for the day!

## Chapter 4: Complete UI Tour

NeoCards is divided into specific screens, or "Pages." You navigate between these pages using the icons on the left side of the screen (the Sidebar). Let's take a tour of every single part of the screen.

### The Title Bar (Top of the window)
- **Purpose**: Allows you to move the window around and control its size.
- **Buttons**: Minimize (hide), Maximize (fill the screen), and Close (X).
- **Shortcuts**: You can double-click the empty space on the title bar to quickly maximize or un-maximize the window.

### The Sidebar (Left side)
This is your main navigation menu. It never disappears.

#### 1. Study Icon (The Book)
- **Purpose**: Takes you to the Home Screen where you can see what you need to study today.
- **When to use it**: Click this when you are ready to start reviewing your flashcards.

#### 2. Add Note Icon (The Plus Sign)
- **Purpose**: Opens the Editor where you write new flashcards.
- **When to use it**: When you learn something new and want to make a flashcard for it.
- **Shortcut**: You can press `Ctrl + N` (or `Cmd + N` on Mac) from anywhere to open this screen.

#### 3. Browser Icon (The Magnifying Glass)
- **Purpose**: Opens a giant spreadsheet of every flashcard you have ever made.
- **When to use it**: When you want to search for a specific card, fix a typo, or organize your cards.

#### 4. Statistics Icon (The Graph)
- **Purpose**: Shows you beautiful charts about how well you are learning and how many days in a row you have studied.
- **When to use it**: When you want to track your progress and stay motivated.

#### 5. AI Copilot Icon (The Robot Brain)
- **Purpose**: Opens the AI Assistant chat panel on the right side of your screen.
- **When to use it**: When you have a long block of text and you want the AI to automatically turn it into flashcards for you.

#### 6. Sync Icon (The Cloud)
- **Purpose**: Backs up your flashcards and sends them to your other devices.
- **When to use it**: Clicking it manually forces a backup, but it also happens automatically in the background.

#### 7. Settings Icon (The Gear)
- **Purpose**: Opens the preferences window where you can change the app theme (Dark/Light mode), adjust the learning math, and manage plugins.

### The Command Palette
- **Purpose**: A hidden search bar that lets you do anything in the app by typing instead of clicking.
- **Why it exists**: Power users can navigate the app much faster using their keyboard.
- **When to use it**: When you want to quickly jump to a specific deck or open a setting without using your mouse.
- **Shortcut**: Press `Ctrl + K` (or `Cmd + K` on Mac). A search bar will pop up in the middle of your screen. Try typing "Dark Mode" and pressing Enter!

### Notifications (Toasts)
- **Purpose**: Small pop-up messages that appear in the bottom right corner of your screen.
- **What they do**: They tell you when a task is successful (e.g., "Note Saved!") or if there is an error (e.g., "Sync Failed: No Internet").
- **When to use it**: You don't interact with them; they just inform you and disappear after 3 seconds.

### Common Mistakes for Beginners
- Do not ignore the Sidebar! Many beginners get lost inside the Browser screen and forget that clicking the "Study" icon (The Book) will take them safely back to the Home screen.
- **Did You Know?**: You can hover your mouse over any icon in the Sidebar for 1 second, and a tiny text box (a tooltip) will appear telling you exactly what the button does and its keyboard shortcut.
